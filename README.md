# geospatial-data-analysis
Here's what the geospatial analysis reveals across four integrated views:

**The choropleth map** shows revenue intensity by state with white dots marking active store locations. Toggle between Revenue, Demand index, and Opportunity gap layers to shift the lens — the gap layer is especially revealing, showing where demand is strong but supply is absent. Hover any state for a full breakdown.

**Six priority expansion targets** surface from the opportunity scoring model (high demand score, ≤3 existing stores). Texas, Florida, and Arizona top the list — large populations, strong demand signals, and still relatively underpenetrated relative to their market size.

**The regional bar chart** shows the projected revenue uplift from full expansion. The Southeast region shows the largest relative gain (+70%), driven by underdeveloped presence in Georgia, Tennessee, and the Carolinas despite solid demand indices across all those states.

**The demand vs. presence bubble chart** is the core strategic tool. States in the top-left quadrant (red bubbles) are the clearest expansion opportunities — high demand, few stores, large markets. States clustering bottom-right are mature markets where further investment yields diminishing returns. The ideal strategic portfolio balances entering top-left targets while defending share in established markets.

Key insight: roughly $2.9B in addressable market remains uncaptured, concentrated in the South and Mountain West. A phased approach — entering 2–3 priority states per year — limits execution risk while capturing the highest-value whitespace first. Hit the roadmap button to build out a detailed 12-month plan.
